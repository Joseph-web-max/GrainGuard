THE RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS

Move from monitoring grain conditions to testing whether an intervention will improve them.

GrainGuard investigates crop-specific deterioration signals and an equilibrium-moisture-aware intervention gate, with explicit uncertainty and operator review.

01 · Problem

Storage loss is multi-factorial

Moisture, temperature gradients, pests, grain damage, condensation, storage history and fungi interact. A single sensor cannot diagnose the whole storage condition. FAO describes moisture, heat and biological factors as interacting causes of loss.

Read FAO post-harvest loss guidance ↗

02 · Existing products

Grain monitoring already exists

Bin-Sense offers temperature/moisture monitoring, alerts, remote access and product tiers with automatic aeration fan control. GrainGuard must not claim to invent grain-bin monitoring or automatic aeration generally.

Review Bin-Sense products ↗

03 · Technical gap

Ventilation can make grain wetter

FAO's controlled-ventilation guidance explains that humid outside air can re-moisten grain. Correct decisions depend on crop equilibrium moisture content, temperature and relative humidity. A simple RH trigger is inadequate.

Read FAO controlled ventilation guidance ↗

04 · Proposed differentiation

Crop-specific intervention gate

Investigate a configurable equilibrium-moisture lookup, local trend analysis, sensor-health evidence and conservative “do not recommend” behaviour when required inputs or validated data are missing. The exact combination requires prior-art and field testing.

05 · Biological limits

CO₂ is not an aflatoxin sensor

Headspace gas trends may help identify abnormal respiration or storage changes, but they cannot prove mycotoxin presence or absence. Aflatoxin status requires appropriate sampling and validated laboratory methods.

Read FAO fungi and mycotoxin guidance ↗

06 · Classification

Research status

Potentially differentiated combination — unverified. Existing monitoring, temperature cables, moisture sensors, aeration controls and grain-quality models are relevant prior art. A claims-based search is required before an IP filing or world-first claim.

What would prove the invention?

Compare it against a standard monitoring-and-rule baseline, not against an imaginary competitor.

01
Sensor calibration / repeatability

Compare each sensor to a traceable reference across the expected operating range. Record calibration date and uncertainty.

02
Moisture meter agreement

Compare crop-specific grain-meter readings with a laboratory reference method; report bias, repeatability and limits of agreement.

03
Ventilation gate false-safe test

Use controlled EMC reference data and independently measured outside air. Count unsafe “go” recommendations as a critical failure.

Storage outcome pilot
